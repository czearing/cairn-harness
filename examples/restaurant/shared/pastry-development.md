# Pastry Development

## Direction

The pastry menu moves from cold, bitter-bright citrus to warm fruit and tender cake, then offers a separate rich chocolate finish a la carte. Fire is reserved for pear and honey, where heat adds depth rather than smoke. Preserved citrus appears only where its salinity or bitterness has a clear balancing role.

## Tasting Menu Course 7

### Citrus and Bay Granita | grapefruit, olive oil, sea salt

**Component specification**

- Pink grapefruit and fresh Meyer lemon granita infused with fresh bay, 45 g
- Grapefruit supremes lightly dressed with grapefruit vinegar, 20 g
- Arbequina olive oil, 4 g
- Flaky sea salt and one tender bay tip

**Key techniques**

- Cold-infuse bay for 30 minutes, then remove it before the base becomes medicinal.
- Balance the granita base to 14 to 16 Brix with a clean, tart finish.
- Freeze in shallow pans and rake repeatedly for dry, distinct crystals.
- Cut supremes to order-day and hold them in their own juice.

**Make-ahead plan**

- Prepare and freeze the granita base up to 3 days ahead. Rake once during freezing and again before service.
- Supreme grapefruit the morning of service.
- Portion oil in squeeze bottles and keep at cool room temperature.

**Pickup sequence**

1. Place chilled grapefruit supremes in a frozen coupe.
2. Rake and mound granita loosely over the fruit.
3. Finish with olive oil, two or three salt flakes, and a bay tip.
4. Serve immediately on a chilled underliner.

**Allergens and adaptation**

- Allergens: none of the major allergens.
- Dairy-free and gluten-free as written. Use dedicated clean pans and utensils for strict gluten-free service.

**Service note**

This course clears the duck with cold acidity and mild bitterness. It contains no smoke and no preserved citrus, preventing the menu's citrus signature from becoming repetitive before dessert.

## Tasting Menu Course 8

### Olive Oil Cake | ember-roasted pear, cultured cream, bay

**Component specification**

- Warm olive oil cake, 45 g
- Ember-roasted Bartlett pear, 35 g
- Lightly whipped cultured cream, 18 g
- Bay and pear reduction, 8 g
- Crisp olive oil tuile, 1 piece

**Key techniques**

- Emulsify fruity olive oil into the batter and bake only until the crumb springs back.
- Roast whole pears beside clean embers, not in smoke, until tender with a lightly blistered skin.
- Rest pears before peeling and portioning so their juices stay in the fruit.
- Reduce pear trimmings with fresh bay, then strain early to keep the bay floral.
- Whip cultured cream to a soft spoonable texture.

**Make-ahead plan**

- Bake the cake up to 1 day ahead; cool, wrap, and portion.
- Roast pears and make the reduction the day of service.
- Mix cultured cream up to 1 day ahead; whip before service.
- Bake tuiles up to 2 days ahead and hold airtight.

**Pickup sequence**

1. Warm cake and pear separately until just hot.
2. Spoon cultured cream slightly off center.
3. Set cake against the cream and arrange pear beside it.
4. Nap pear with warm bay reduction and add the tuile at the pass.

**Allergens and adaptation**

- Allergens: wheat, egg, milk.
- Gluten-free adaptation: replace flour with the house gluten-free cake blend and bake in dedicated molds. Confirm gluten-free baking powder and tuile; use a crisp buckwheat wafer in place of the wheat tuile.
- Dairy-free adaptation: replace cultured cream with whipped oat creme fraiche. The cake and pear components are dairy-free as written.

**Service note**

The portion remains modest and the cream tangy, giving the tasting menu a warm, rounded finish without heaviness. The live-fire pear is the sole fire-driven pastry component in the tasting progression.

## A La Carte Dessert 13

### Citrus and Bay Granita | grapefruit, olive oil, sea salt

**Component specification**

- Grapefruit and Meyer lemon granita, 70 g
- Fresh grapefruit and Cara Cara orange segments, 45 g
- Olive oil cake crumb toasted with sea salt, 12 g
- Arbequina olive oil, 5 g
- Fresh bay sugar, a light dusting

**Key techniques**

- Use the tasting-menu granita base and keep the crystals loose.
- Macerate citrus for no more than 10 minutes to preserve clean texture.
- Toast cake crumb until dry and crisp, using tasting cake trim to reduce waste.
- Grind bay into sugar immediately before service for a fresh aroma.

**Make-ahead plan**

- Use granita held under the tasting-menu plan.
- Segment fruit the morning of service and keep varieties separate.
- Toast cake crumb daily and hold airtight.
- Blend bay sugar in small daily batches.

**Pickup sequence**

1. Arrange citrus in a chilled bowl.
2. Add a broad mound of granita.
3. Scatter cake crumb around, keeping some dry.
4. Finish with olive oil, sea salt, and a restrained dusting of bay sugar.

**Allergens and adaptation**

- Allergens: wheat and egg from the cake crumb.
- Gluten-free adaptation: use toasted gluten-free olive oil cake crumb from the tasting adaptation.
- Dairy-free as written.

**Distinct role**

This is the lightest a la carte finish: icy, juicy, tart, and crisp. The cake crumb gives it more substance than the tasting pre-dessert without turning it creamy.

## A La Carte Dessert 14

### Olive Oil Cake | ember-roasted pear, cultured cream, bay

**Component specification**

- Warm olive oil cake, 75 g
- Ember-roasted pear, 60 g
- Cultured cream, 30 g
- Bay and pear reduction, 12 g
- Candied walnut, 15 g

**Key techniques**

- Use the tasting-menu cake, pear, cream, and reduction methods.
- Candy walnuts to a thin, dry shell and season with sea salt.
- Warm the cake in a covered pan so it stays moist.

**Make-ahead plan**

- Share cake, pear, cream, and reduction mise en place with tasting dessert.
- Candy walnuts up to 3 days ahead and hold airtight.
- Reserve larger, intact pear pieces for a la carte plating.

**Pickup sequence**

1. Warm cake, pear, and reduction.
2. Spoon cultured cream onto a warm plate.
3. Place cake and pear with visible separation.
4. Add reduction and candied walnuts immediately before sending.

**Allergens and adaptation**

- Allergens: wheat, egg, milk, walnut.
- Gluten-free adaptation: use the dedicated gluten-free cake and buckwheat crisp process specified for the tasting dessert. Walnuts are naturally gluten-free but require segregated handling.
- Dairy-free adaptation: substitute whipped oat creme fraiche for cultured cream.

**Distinct role**

This is the fruit-led, medium-rich option: warm and tender with cool tang and hard walnut crunch. Its fire character comes from caramelized pear, with no added smoke.

## A La Carte Dessert 15

### Dark Chocolate Tart | burnt honey, mandarin, cocoa nib

**Component specification**

- Cocoa shortcrust tart shell, 1 individual shell
- 70 percent dark chocolate ganache, 55 g
- Burnt honey caramel, 14 g
- Fresh mandarin curd, 18 g
- Cocoa nib and sesame praline, 12 g
- Mandarin segments dressed with preserved mandarin brine, 25 g

**Key techniques**

- Blind-bake tart shells until fully crisp and seal with tempered dark chocolate.
- Emulsify ganache without aeration for a clean, dense slice.
- Cook honey to deep amber, arrest with hot cream, and season with salt.
- Cook curd gently and strain for a bright counterpoint to the ganache.
- Use preserved mandarin brine by drops only; it should sharpen the fresh fruit, not read as saline.

**Make-ahead plan**

- Bake and seal shells up to 2 days ahead; hold dry.
- Fill tarts with ganache up to 1 day ahead and refrigerate covered.
- Prepare caramel and curd up to 3 days ahead.
- Make praline up to 4 days ahead and hold airtight.
- Segment and dress mandarins on the day of service.

**Pickup sequence**

1. Temper the filled tart for 8 to 10 minutes before plating.
2. Pipe mandarin curd beside the tart.
3. Spoon warm burnt honey caramel across one edge.
4. Add dressed mandarin segments and finish with praline.

**Allergens and adaptation**

- Allergens: wheat, milk, egg, sesame. Confirm soy content in chocolate.
- Gluten-free adaptation: use a cocoa-almond tart shell made and baked with dedicated equipment; adds almond as an allergen.
- Dairy-free adaptation: use oat cream in ganache and caramel, certified dairy-free dark chocolate, and plant butter in the shell and curd.

**Distinct role**

This is the richest finish: cool dense chocolate, crisp pastry, sticky bitter caramel, juicy mandarin, and brittle praline. Preserved citrus is used only to season the fresh fruit and cut the chocolate.

## Cross-Utilization and Progression Check

| Dish | Dominant flavor | Primary texture and temperature | Purposeful signature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tasting granita | Tart grapefruit and floral bay | Icy, juicy, cold | Olive oil links the coastal pantry without fire or preservation |
| Tasting olive oil cake | Pear and olive oil | Tender, creamy, crisp, warm and cool | Pear is roasted beside clean embers |
| A la carte granita | Mixed citrus | Icy, juicy, crumbly, cold | Cake trim reduces waste |
| A la carte olive oil cake | Fire-caramelized pear | Tender, creamy, crunchy, warm and cool | Ember roasting is central but unsmoked |
| Dark chocolate tart | Dark chocolate and bitter honey | Dense, crisp, sticky, juicy | Preserved mandarin brine balances richness |

Shared granita, cake, pear, cultured cream, and bay reduction reduce prep load. Different portions, garnishes, and textures keep the tasting and a la carte presentations distinct. The progression remains light to rich, and no dessert uses smoke as a primary flavor.
