## Tasting progression

The current order regresses from earthy, smoky beet with yogurt and nuts at
course 2 to the more delicate scallop at course 3. Use this corrected order:

1. Pacific oyster, grilled cucumber, preserved Meyer lemon, sea herbs
2. Diver scallop, charred mandarin, fennel, Fresno chile
3. Ember-baked beet, smoked yogurt, pistachio, rose vinegar
4. Black cod, shellfish broth, grilled leek, lemon kosho
5. Fire-roasted artichoke, white bean miso, nettle, sourdough crumb
6. Dry-aged duck, sour cherry, smoked onion, duck jus
7. Citrus and bay granita, grapefruit, olive oil, sea salt
8. Olive oil cake, ember-roasted pear, cultured cream, bay tuile

This rises from briny and delicate through seared shellfish, earthy produce,
rich fish, hearth vegetable, and duck, then resets with ice before the richer
cake. The revised citrus sequence is courses 1 and 2, a break at course 3,
course 4, then a two-course break before course 7. No run exceeds 2 courses.

## Repetition, contrast, and menu identity

All 8 tasting courses currently reappear almost unchanged a la carte. This is
efficient, but it fails the requirement that cross-utilized components not make
dishes taste repetitive and gives the tasting menu no exclusive development.
Keep the shared mise en place, but differentiate at least these pickups:

- Serve the tasting scallop as one small, gently seared scallop with fennel
  broth and mandarin kosho; keep the a la carte scallops as a larger hard-seared
  portion with charred mandarin and shaved fennel.
- Use warm beet, rose vinegar, and a measured smoked-yogurt sauce in the tasting
  course; retain blood orange only on the larger a la carte beet dish.
- Serve tasting black cod with shellfish broth and crisp leek; make the a la
  carte cod crisp-skinned with grilled leek and a reduced broth.
- Serve tasting artichoke as a small heart with nettle broth; make the a la
  carte artichoke a shareable roasted half with white bean miso and crumb.
- Use sliced duck with jus in the tasting menu; serve a whole crisp-skinned
  breast a la carte.
- Churn the tasting pre-dessert to order from a shallow granita pan; give the a
  la carte version a composed grapefruit format rather than the identical
  tasting portion.

Texture and temperature contrast is credible in most dishes, but several need
their pickup language fixed. Specify crisp skin on black cod and duck, raw or
crisp fennel with scallop, toasted crumb on artichoke, and a bay tuile on olive
oil cake. Without those details, cod, duck, and cake read as collections of soft
components.

Preserved citrus currently appears on 11 of 15 a la carte dishes. Reduce that
to 8 by removing citrus ash from hearth bread, replacing blood orange on the a
la carte beet with a tart non-citrus finish if the tasting beet retains orange,
and removing mandarin from the chocolate tart. Suggested replacements are
charred-allium ash on bread, rose vinegar on one beet format, and chicory
caramel on the tart. This preserves the specialty while allowing dairy, cocoa,
smoke, herbs, and fermentation to register independently.

## Smoke and fire fatigue

Explicit smoke is limited to yogurt and onion, but charred, grilled, fire,
coal, or ember treatments dominate the middle of the tasting and most savory
a la carte pickups. Keep smoke only in the beet yogurt and duck onion. Do not
smoke broths, miso, fruit, butter, or jus. Use clean shellfish and herb broths,
raw fennel, fresh herbs, and rose vinegar as palate relief. The granita resets
the palate before ember-roasted pear, so the final fire reference is acceptable.

## Cross-utilization

Useful shared mise en place includes grilled cucumber between oyster formats,
mandarin kosho between crispy rice and scallop, white bean miso and nettle
between artichoke formats, shellfish trim in black-cod broth, duck trim in jus,
and preserved-citrus brines as seasoning bases. Consolidate the current lemon,
Meyer lemon, mandarin, kumquat, grapefruit, blood orange, and generic preserved
citrus preparations into no more than 4 base processes: lemon brine, mandarin
kosho, kumquat preserve, and grapefruit granita base. Derive mignonette and
broth seasoning from those bases rather than maintaining single-dish citrus
SKUs.

Do not cross-utilize finished sauces across dishes without changing their role.
For example, mandarin kosho may season cold crab and a warm scallop broth, but
the visible garnish and dilution must differ. The tasting and a la carte
versions also need the format distinctions listed above.

## Pickup complexity, holding risks, and bottlenecks

The primary bottleneck is the live-fire station. Bread, beets, cabbage,
artichoke, scallops, rockfish, quail, duck, and lamb can converge there during
a la carte service while the tasting menu calls for sequential fire pickups.
Move beets and artichokes to advance roasting with a short finishing char, bake
and rewarm bread off the main grill, sear scallops on plancha, and assign whole
rockfish and meat to separate grill zones. Cap whole-rockfish orders per
15-minute window or quote an explicit cook time.

The second bottleneck is sauce finishing. Shellfish broth, herb broth, duck
jus, smoked yogurt, white bean miso, and multiple citrus condiments create
simultaneous hot and cold sauce work. Hold only broth and jus hot in small
batches; keep yogurt, miso, and kosho cold; finish broth with herb oil instead
of butter. Use one dedicated saucier during peak service.

Specific holding controls:

- Shuck oysters to order. Hold drained cucumber separately for no more than one
  service and dress at pickup.
- Hold crispy rice dry and uncovered; add cold crab only after frying or
  reheating so it does not soften.
- Advance-roast beets and artichokes, but finish the same service. Acidulate
  artichokes against oxidation and add crumb only at the pass.
- Prepare cabbage in portioned wedges and finish to order. Do not hot-hold it,
  which collapses its texture.
- Cook scallop, cod, quail, duck, lamb, and whole rockfish to order. Standardize
  rockfish size, quail brine, duck tempering, and meat resting times.
- Hold nettle sauce cold and reheat briefly to protect color. Hold shellfish
  broth, herb broth, and duck jus in small batches within the kitchen's
  validated food-safety limits, not across services.
- Rake granita frequently in shallow pans and discard degraded, refrozen
  crystals. Hold cake wrapped, pear in its liquor, tuile dry, and plate cream
  cold at pickup.

### Revised tasting correction

Do not reorder scallop and beet. The developed sauces make the existing arc
work if course 2 is corrected: oyster is cold and saline, beet is earthy and
smoky, scallop is warm and bright, cod is savory and broth-led, artichoke is
hearth-cooked, and duck is the centerpiece. Reordering would place two delicate
seafood courses first and require unnecessary changes to established sauce
production.

Instead, replace tasting course 2 blood orange agrodolce with pomegranate
agrodolce at the same finished consistency and acidity. Keep pistachio and
smoked yogurt. This single correction breaks the four-course citrus run while
retaining color and contrast. Courses 1, 3, and 4 still use different citrus
forms, but course 4 kosho must remain the developed 2 g isolated accent rather
than being dissolved into broth.

Add fried leek root or a trim-driven potato crisp to black cod and duck-skin
crackling to duck. These correct the soft-on-soft textures without adding major
prep families. Retain fine crisp cucumber dice on both oyster formats.

### Final allergen corrections

The service matrix must carry complete-plate allergens rather than
component-only notes:

- Oyster and scallop are molluscan shellfish.
- Crispy rice contains crustacean shellfish and egg, with possible soy from
  mayonnaise.
- Black cod contains fish, crustacean shellfish, and sulfites as developed.
- Artichoke contains soy and wheat, with possible milk from sourced sourdough.
- Quail contains sesame.
- A la carte granita contains wheat and egg from cake crumb, unlike the tasting
  granita.
- A la carte olive oil cake contains wheat, egg, milk, and walnut.
- Chocolate tart contains wheat, milk, egg, and sesame, with soy to be verified
  from chocolate. Its gluten-free shell introduces almond.
- Vermouth and wine vinegars add sulfites to relevant broths, jus, gastriques,
  relishes, and sauces.

Prepare shellfish-free stock, gluten-free crumbs and cake, dairy-free creams,
and egg-free mayonnaise as separate labeled batches. None is safe as a garnish
removal at pickup.

### Final holding and bottleneck corrections

The specialist limits are usable, but execution depends on station controls:

- Split shellfish, fennel, fish, poultry, and lamb liquids into small service
  batches. Hold no longer than 3 hours at 150 F or above and never carry hot
  shellfish broth into another service.
- Date sea-herb, nettle, mint, scallion, leek, and general green-herb oils
  separately. Chill them immediately and never combine old and new batches.
- Combine pistachio relish no more than 30 minutes before service and discard
  after its 4-hour limit. Shake mandarin vinaigrette at pickup. Never freeze
  sunflower tahini or mandarin-kosho mayonnaise.
- Assign hearth zones and opening order caps for whole rockfish, quail, duck,
  and lamb. Par-roast beets and artichokes, then finish over fire. Quote whole
  fish cook time explicitly.
- Temper a forecast number of chocolate tarts on a timed rail for their 8 to
  10-minute pickup. Warm cake and pear separately. Rake granita immediately
  before plating and send it without holding at the pass.
