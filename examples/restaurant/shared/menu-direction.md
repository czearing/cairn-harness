# Menu Direction

## Concept

The restaurant is a contemporary coastal California dining room built around live-fire cooking. The food should feel bright, generous, and precise rather than rustic or smoke-heavy.

## Specialty

The signature is **coastal ingredients cooked over embers and balanced with house-preserved citrus**. Fire supplies char, warmth, and aroma. Preserved citrus supplies acidity, salinity, and continuity across the menu.

## Core Ingredients and Techniques

- Pacific seafood, seasonal California produce, duck, lamb, and quail
- Meyer lemon, mandarin, kumquat, and grapefruit preserved as kosho, brine, vinegar, or marmalade
- Ember roasting, hearth grilling, coal baking, and light smoking
- Fermented accents such as white bean miso, cultured dairy, and fruit vinegar
- Fresh herbs, restrained chile, and clean broths to prevent smoke fatigue

## Shared Constraints

- Smoke is an accent, not the dominant flavor.
- Every savory dish must include freshness or acidity.
- No course may repeat the same primary ingredient.
- The tasting menu must rise from raw and delicate food to the hearth-cooked centerpiece, then resolve with two light-to-rich dessert steps.
- The a la carte menu must contain exactly 15 dishes: 3 snacks, 3 vegetables, 3 seafood dishes, 3 meat dishes, and 3 desserts.
- At least 5 a la carte dishes must be vegetarian, including desserts.
- At least 3 savory a la carte dishes must be adaptable without dairy.
- Preserved citrus should appear in multiple forms, but no more than 2 adjacent tasting courses.
- Components should cross-utilize without making dishes taste repetitive.
- Dish descriptions should name the primary ingredient and no more than 3 supporting elements.
- Allergens, dietary adaptations, and advance preparation risks must be identified before final approval.

## Acceptance Criteria

- Both menus clearly express the live-fire and preserved-citrus specialty.
- The tasting menu contains 8 courses with a deliberate progression and no heavy finish.
- The a la carte menu contains exactly 15 distinct dishes in the required category mix.
- Each dish has contrast in temperature, texture, richness, or acidity.
- Specialist sauce and pastry work fits the shared direction and is attached in full to handoff messages.
- Recipe testing flags operational bottlenecks, allergen issues, weak transitions, and repeated flavors.
